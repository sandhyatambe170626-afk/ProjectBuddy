Working Model of a Hydraulic Lift (Pascal’s Law)

Back

Aim / Objective

To demonstrate Pascal’s Law using a simple hydraulic lift model made with syringes and water.

Concept

Pascal’s Law states that pressure applied to a confined liquid is transmitted equally in all directions. When we press a small syringe, the pressure travels through the water and lifts a heavier object connected to a bigger syringe.

Real-life Applications:

  • Car service station lifts
  • Hydraulic brakes in vehicles
  • Construction machines like excavators and cranes

Materials Required

  • 2 plastic syringes (one small, one large)
  • Plastic tube (to connect syringes)
  • Water (can add food color for visibility)
  • Cardboard base
  • Glue / Tape
  • Small platform (thermocol/cardboard piece)
  • Heavy object (book, toy, etc.)

Steps to Make the Model

  1. Take two syringes of different sizes.
  2. Fill both syringes completely with water (no air bubbles).
  3. Connect both syringes using a plastic tube.
  4. Fix the larger syringe vertically on a cardboard base.
  5. Attach a small platform on top of the larger syringe piston.
  6. Place a heavy object on the platform.
  7. Press the smaller syringe slowly and observe.

How It Works

When you press the smaller syringe, pressure is created in the water. According to Pascal’s Law, this pressure is transmitted equally through the liquid. The larger syringe has a bigger area, so it produces more force. This force lifts the heavy object placed on it.

Formula:

Pressure = Force / Area

Since pressure is equal in both syringes: F1 / A1 = F2 / A2

If A2 is bigger, then F2 becomes bigger — so heavy objects can be lifted easily.

Result

When pressure is applied to the smaller syringe, the larger syringe moves upward and lifts the heavy object. This proves Pascal’s Law — pressure in a liquid is transmitted equally in all directions.

Conclusion

The hydraulic lift model successfully demonstrates how a small force can lift heavy loads using fluid pressure. This principle is widely used in real-life machines and vehicles.
