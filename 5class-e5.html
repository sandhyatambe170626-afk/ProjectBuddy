Save Mangroves – Protect Coastal Areas

Back

Introduction

Mangroves are special types of trees that grow in salty coastal water. They are commonly found in tropical and subtropical regions where rivers meet the sea.

In India, mangroves are mainly found in the Sundarbans, Mahanadi Delta, Godavari-Krishna Delta, and along the coast of Maharashtra.

Mangroves protect coastal areas from cyclones, floods, and soil erosion. They act as natural barriers against strong waves and storms. Mangrove forests also provide shelter to many animals such as fish, crabs, birds, and other marine species.

However, mangroves are being destroyed due to pollution, construction, and urban development. Saving mangroves is very important for protecting our environment and coastal communities.

Objective

  • To understand the importance of mangroves.
  • To show how mangroves protect coastal areas from natural disasters.
  • To spread awareness about conserving mangrove forests.
  • To suggest ways to protect and restore mangroves.

Concepts Used

  • Ecosystem and Biodiversity
  • Soil Erosion
  • Coastal Protection
  • Natural Disaster Management
  • Climate Change Control

Materials Required

  • Thermocol or cardboard base
  • Brown and green clay
  • Blue chart paper (for sea)
  • Small sticks (tree stems)
  • Cotton (for clouds)
  • Toy houses or boats (optional)
  • Labels
  • Fevicol or glue
  • Poster colors
  • Transparent plastic sheet (for waves)

Steps to Make the Model

  1. Divide the base into two sections: one with mangroves and one without mangroves.
  2. Use blue chart paper to create the sea.
  3. Make waves using white paint or plastic sheet.
  4. Create mangrove trees using sticks as trunks and green clay for leaves.
  5. Spread roots around the base to show the unique mangrove root system.
  6. On the "without mangroves" side, show soil erosion and damaged houses.
  7. On the "with mangroves" side, show protected houses and calm sea waves.
  8. Add labels such as "Storm Protection", "Soil Erosion", and "Fish Habitat".

How It Works

Mangroves have strong roots that spread widely in the soil and water. These roots help hold the soil together and prevent coastal erosion.

  • Mangroves reduce the impact of strong waves and storms.
  • The roots trap sediments and stabilize the shoreline.
  • They provide shelter and breeding grounds for many marine animals.
  • Mangroves also absorb carbon dioxide and help reduce global warming.

Expected Result

  • The model shows the difference between coastal areas with and without mangroves.
  • Students understand how mangroves protect coastal regions.
  • The project spreads awareness about the importance of conserving mangrove forests.

Conclusion

Mangroves are nature’s shield for coastal areas. They protect people, animals, and the environment from natural disasters like cyclones and floods.

Protecting mangroves is essential for maintaining ecological balance and ensuring a safer future for coastal communities.
