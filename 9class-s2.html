Biodegradable Plastic from Milk

Back

Aim

  • To prepare biodegradable plastic from milk using casein protein.
  • To understand the concept of polymers in chemistry.

Concept Used

  • Polymers – large molecules made of repeating units.
  • Casein Protein – milk protein that forms plastic-like material.
  • Acid-Base Reaction.
  • Eco-friendly materials and sustainability.

Ingredients Required

  • 1 cup milk (preferably full cream)
  • 2–3 tablespoons vinegar or lemon juice
  • Strainer
  • Bowl
  • Spoon
  • Butter paper or aluminum foil
  • Food color (optional)
  • Cookie cutters or molds (optional)

Steps to Make the Model

  1. Heat the Milk – Pour milk into a pan and heat it without boiling fully.
  2. Add Vinegar or Lemon Juice – Slowly add vinegar while stirring. The milk will start curdling.
  3. Strain the Mixture – Use a strainer to separate the solid curd from liquid whey.
  4. Wash the Curd – Rinse with clean water to remove extra acid smell.
  5. Add Color (Optional) – Mix food color to create colored plastic.
  6. Shape the Plastic – Press the curd into molds or shape it by hand.
  7. Dry the Material – Keep it on butter paper and allow it to dry for 1–2 days.

How It Works

Milk contains a protein called casein. When vinegar (acid) is added to hot milk, the casein separates from the liquid part of milk.

  • The casein molecules join together.
  • They form long molecular chains called polymers.
  • After drying, these chains harden and form a plastic-like material.

This plastic is natural and biodegradable, which means it can decompose in soil unlike synthetic plastic.

Result

  • A hard plastic-like material is formed from milk.
  • The material can be molded into different shapes.
  • The produced plastic is biodegradable and eco-friendly.

Conclusion

This experiment shows how natural materials like milk can be used to create biodegradable plastic. It helps students understand polymers, sustainable materials, and eco-friendly alternatives to synthetic plastic.
