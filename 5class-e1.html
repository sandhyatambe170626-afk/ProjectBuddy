Solar Energy vs Electricity from Coal

Back

Introduction

Electricity is the backbone of modern development. In countries like India, most electricity is still generated from coal-based thermal power plants. Coal is a fossil fuel formed over millions of years and is a non-renewable resource.

Burning coal releases harmful gases such as carbon dioxide (CO₂), sulfur dioxide (SO₂), and nitrogen oxides (NOₓ), which contribute to global warming and air pollution.

On the other hand, solar energy is a renewable and clean source of power derived from sunlight. India is rapidly expanding solar infrastructure through large projects like the Bhadla Solar Park.

This project compares the environmental, economic, and sustainability aspects of solar energy and coal-based electricity to understand which energy source is more suitable for the future.

Objective

  • To compare renewable and non-renewable energy sources.
  • To analyze environmental impact of coal vs solar energy.
  • To understand working principles of thermal and solar power plants.
  • To promote sustainable energy solutions.

Concepts Used

  • Renewable Energy
  • Non-Renewable Energy
  • Solar Energy Technology
  • Thermal Power Generation
  • Environmental Sustainability

Materials Required

  • Thermocol base (2–3 feet)
  • Cardboard sheets
  • Black chart paper
  • Yellow/orange paper (for Sun model)
  • Small DC motor (for turbine demo)
  • LED bulb
  • 9V battery
  • Switch
  • Small solar panel (optional)
  • Cotton (for smoke effect)
  • Printed labels and arrows

Steps to Make the Model

  1. Divide the base into two sections: Coal Power Plant and Solar Energy System.
  2. Create a factory building and chimney using cardboard for the coal plant.
  3. Add cotton at the chimney top to represent smoke pollution.
  4. Attach a small DC motor to represent the turbine and connect an LED to show electricity generation.
  5. On the other side, make a large sun using yellow paper.
  6. Create tilted solar panels using reflective blue sheet or cardboard.
  7. Connect a small solar panel (or battery switch system) to light an LED in a small house model.
  8. Add labels showing the energy flow in both systems.

How It Works

Coal-Based Thermal Power Plant

  • Coal is burned in a boiler.
  • Heat converts water into high-pressure steam.
  • Steam rotates a turbine.
  • The turbine rotates a generator.
  • The generator produces electricity.
  • Smoke containing CO₂ and pollutants is released.

Solar Power Plant (Photovoltaic System)

  • Sunlight hits solar panels.
  • Photons excite electrons in silicon cells.
  • Direct current (DC) electricity is produced.
  • An inverter converts DC to AC.
  • Electricity powers homes or stores in batteries.

Expected Result

  • Coal plant section shows electricity generation with visible pollution.
  • Solar plant section shows clean electricity generation.
  • Students can visually compare pollution vs clean energy.

Conclusion

Coal has powered industries for many decades, but it causes severe environmental damage and contributes to climate change. Solar energy provides a cleaner, renewable alternative that produces electricity without pollution during operation.

This project helps students understand the importance of switching to sustainable energy sources and encourages the use of solar energy for a greener future.
