Working Model of Human Lungs

Back

Introduction

The human lungs are important organs of the respiratory system. They help us breathe in oxygen and breathe out carbon dioxide.

Respiration is the process by which our body takes in oxygen and removes carbon dioxide. In this project, we create a simple working model to demonstrate how the lungs expand and contract during breathing.

This model helps students understand the breathing process in a simple and practical way.

Aim of the Project

To demonstrate the process of inhalation and exhalation using a simple lung model made with balloons and a plastic bottle.

Materials Required

  • 1 transparent plastic bottle (1–2 litre)
  • 2 small balloons (same size)
  • 1 large balloon
  • 2 straws (or Y-shaped straw)
  • Rubber band
  • Tape
  • Scissors or cutter (use with adult help)
  • Clay or glue (to seal gaps)

Step-by-Step Procedure

  1. Cut the bottom part of the plastic bottle and remove it.
  2. Tie two small balloons to the ends of two straws (or a Y-shaped straw).
  3. Insert the straws through the mouth of the bottle so that the balloons hang inside.
  4. Seal the bottle opening with clay or tape so that air cannot enter.
  5. Stretch the large balloon over the cut bottom of the bottle and secure it with a rubber band.
  6. Pull the large balloon gently down and push it up to observe how the model works.

How It Works

  • The small balloons represent the lungs.
  • The straw represents the trachea (windpipe).
  • The plastic bottle represents the chest cavity.
  • The large balloon at the bottom represents the diaphragm.

When You Pull the Large Balloon Down

  • The space inside the bottle increases.
  • Air enters the small balloons.
  • The balloons inflate.
  • This represents inhalation (breathing in).

When You Push the Large Balloon Up

  • The space inside the bottle decreases.
  • Air moves out of the small balloons.
  • The balloons deflate.
  • This represents exhalation (breathing out).

Result

The model successfully demonstrates how the lungs expand during inhalation and contract during exhalation with the movement of the diaphragm.

Conclusion

This working model clearly explains the process of respiration in humans. It helps students understand how breathing works in a simple and practical way.
