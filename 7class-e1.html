Urban Heat Island Effect

Back

Introduction

The Urban Heat Island Effect happens when cities become hotter than nearby rural areas. This occurs because buildings, concrete roads, vehicles, and pollution absorb and trap heat.

In contrast, villages and green areas have trees, soil, and water bodies which help keep the temperature cooler. Urbanization reduces natural vegetation and increases surfaces that store heat.

This project helps students understand how cities become warmer than villages and how green spaces help reduce temperature.

Objective

  • To demonstrate why cities are hotter than villages.
  • To understand the concept of the Urban Heat Island Effect.
  • To study the role of trees and green spaces in reducing temperature.
  • To create awareness about sustainable urban development.

Concepts Used

  • Urban Heat Island Effect
  • Urbanization and Climate
  • Heat Absorption by Concrete
  • Role of Trees and Green Spaces
  • Environmental Sustainability

Materials Required

  • Cardboard or thermocol base
  • Small cardboard boxes (for buildings)
  • Black chart paper (for roads)
  • Green chart paper or artificial grass
  • Small plant models or paper trees
  • Toy cars (optional)
  • Glue or Fevicol
  • Scissors
  • Cotton (for clouds)
  • Sketch pens or markers
  • Labels for city and village areas

Steps to Make the Model

  1. Prepare the base using a cardboard sheet and divide it into two parts: city area and village area.
  2. Create the city area using cardboard boxes as buildings and black chart paper as roads. Add toy cars and very few trees.
  3. Create the village or green area using green paper to represent grass and add many trees and plants. Draw or place a small pond or river.
  4. Show the heat effect by drawing sun rays. On the city side draw red heat waves, while on the village side show cool shade under trees.
  5. Add labels such as “City Area – High Temperature”, “Village Area – Low Temperature”, and “Trees Reduce Heat”.

How It Works

Concrete, asphalt roads, and buildings absorb sunlight during the day and store heat. At night they slowly release this heat, making cities warmer than nearby rural areas.

  • Urban areas have more buildings and less greenery.
  • Concrete surfaces trap heat and increase temperature.
  • Trees provide shade and cool the air through evaporation.
  • Green spaces help reduce the Urban Heat Island Effect.

Expected Result

  • The model clearly shows the temperature difference between city and village areas.
  • Students understand how buildings and roads increase heat.
  • The project highlights the importance of trees and green spaces in cities.

Conclusion

The Urban Heat Island Effect shows how rapid urbanization can increase temperatures in cities. Concrete buildings and roads absorb and store heat, making urban areas warmer than rural areas.

Increasing green spaces, planting more trees, and using eco-friendly building materials can help reduce heat and make cities healthier places to live.
