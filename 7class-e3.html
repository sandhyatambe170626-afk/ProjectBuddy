Introduction

A forest ecosystem is a natural system where living organisms such as plants, animals, birds, insects, and microorganisms interact with non-living components like soil, water, air, and sunlight.

Forests are often called the “lungs of the Earth” because they absorb carbon dioxide and release oxygen through photosynthesis. They provide food, shelter, and habitat for many living organisms.

This project helps students understand how plants, animals, decomposers, and natural resources work together to maintain ecological balance in a forest ecosystem.

Objective

  • To study the structure of a forest ecosystem.
  • To understand the interaction between living and non-living components.
  • To learn about producers, consumers, and decomposers.
  • To create awareness about forest conservation.

Concepts Used

  • Ecosystem
  • Photosynthesis
  • Food Chain and Food Web
  • Nutrient Cycle
  • Carbon and Oxygen Cycle
  • Decomposers

Components of Forest Ecosystem

Producers (Plants and Trees)

  • Plants make their own food using sunlight through photosynthesis.
  • They provide oxygen and food for animals.

Consumers (Animals and Birds)

  • Herbivores eat plants (deer, rabbit).
  • Carnivores eat other animals (tiger, lion).
  • Omnivores eat both plants and animals.

Decomposers (Bacteria and Fungi)

  • They break down dead plants and animals.
  • They return nutrients back to the soil.

Abiotic Components

  • Sunlight
  • Water
  • Soil
  • Air

Materials Required

  • Cardboard base
  • Green and brown chart paper
  • Blue paper (for river)
  • Cotton (for clouds)
  • Clay or thermocol
  • Small toy animals (optional)
  • Glue and scissors
  • Sketch pens
  • Labels

Steps to Make the Model

  1. Prepare the base by covering cardboard with green chart paper to represent land.
  2. Create trees using paper rolls for trunks and green paper for leaves.
  3. Add a river using blue chart paper and shiny plastic sheet for water effect.
  4. Draw the sun in one corner and use cotton to create clouds.
  5. Add animals such as deer, monkeys, birds, and tiger using toys or drawings.
  6. Show decomposers by placing dry leaves under trees and labeling fungi and bacteria.
  7. Add arrows and labels such as sunlight, photosynthesis, oxygen, carbon dioxide, soil, water, nutrients, and decomposers.

How It Works

A forest ecosystem works through a natural cycle where energy and nutrients move between plants, animals, and microorganisms.

  • Sunlight provides energy for plants.
  • Plants produce food through photosynthesis.
  • Animals eat plants or other animals.
  • When plants and animals die, decomposers break them down.
  • Nutrients return to the soil and help new plants grow.

Expected Result

  • The model demonstrates the interaction between plants, animals, and the environment.
  • Students understand the food chain and nutrient cycle in forests.
  • The project highlights the importance of forests for maintaining ecological balance.

Conclusion

Forests are very important for maintaining oxygen balance, climate control, biodiversity, and soil fertility. They support a wide variety of plants and animals.

Protecting forests by avoiding deforestation and planting more trees is essential for maintaining a healthy and sustainable environment.
