Greywater Recycling System

Back

Introduction

Water is one of the most valuable natural resources on Earth. Due to rapid urbanization, population growth, and climate change, many cities are facing serious water shortages.

A large amount of household water is wasted every day in the form of greywater. Greywater is wastewater generated from bathrooms, washing machines, and kitchen sinks (excluding toilet waste).

Instead of letting this water flow directly into drains, it can be filtered and reused for gardening, cleaning, and toilet flushing. A greywater recycling system helps conserve fresh water and reduce sewage load.

Objective

  • To understand the concept of greywater recycling.
  • To demonstrate a simple water filtration system.
  • To create awareness about water conservation.
  • To show how wastewater can be safely reused.

Concepts Used

  • Water Conservation
  • Filtration Process
  • Wastewater Recycling
  • Sustainable Water Management
  • Environmental Protection

Materials Required

  • 3–4 transparent plastic bottles
  • Small stones (gravel)
  • Sand
  • Charcoal pieces
  • Cotton cloth
  • Plastic pipes or straws
  • Small tap (optional)
  • Cardboard or thermocol base
  • Glue gun
  • Artificial plants (for demonstration)

Steps to Make the Model

  1. Create a collection tank using a plastic bottle and label it "Greywater Collection Tank".
  2. Pour colored water into the tank to represent used water.
  3. Cut another bottle and arrange filtration layers inside it.
  4. Place stones at the bottom, sand above it, charcoal above sand, and cloth at the top.
  5. Connect a pipe from the collection tank to the filtration bottle.
  6. Place another container below the filter to collect cleaned water.
  7. Add a small tap to show water reuse.
  8. Show plants being watered with the filtered water.

How It Works

Greywater recycling works on the principle of filtration and reuse.

  • Greywater first enters the collection tank.
  • It flows into the filtration chamber.
  • Gravel removes large particles.
  • Sand removes fine particles.
  • Charcoal removes odor and improves water clarity.
  • The filtered water is collected and reused for gardening or cleaning.

Expected Result

  • Dirty greywater passes through filtration layers.
  • Impurities are removed through gravel, sand, and charcoal.
  • Filtered water becomes suitable for non-drinking uses like watering plants.

Conclusion

Greywater recycling is an effective method to conserve water and reduce waste. By filtering and reusing wastewater for activities like gardening and cleaning, we can reduce freshwater consumption.

This project encourages people to adopt simple water-saving techniques at home and helps promote sustainable water management.
