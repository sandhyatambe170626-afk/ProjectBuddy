Air Purifying Plants at Home

Back

Introduction

Air pollution is one of the major environmental problems in cities. Indoor air pollution is caused by dust, smoke, perfumes, chemicals from paints, cleaning sprays, and electronic appliances.

According to research by NASA, certain indoor plants can absorb harmful gases like carbon dioxide, benzene, formaldehyde, and carbon monoxide.

This project demonstrates how air-purifying plants can improve indoor air quality naturally and reduce health problems such as allergies, asthma, and headaches.

Objective

  • To study how indoor plants help purify air.
  • To demonstrate natural air purification using plants.
  • To create awareness about indoor air pollution.
  • To promote eco-friendly and healthy living.

Concepts Used

  • Photosynthesis
  • Indoor Air Pollution
  • Natural Air Purification
  • Environmental Science
  • Sustainable Living

Materials Required

  • Thermocol or cardboard base
  • Colored paper
  • Small artificial or real plants
  • Labels for plants
  • Cotton (for polluted air effect)
  • Chart paper
  • Glue and scissors
  • Small plastic house model or handmade room

Steps to Make the Model

  1. Create a base using thermocol or cardboard.
  2. Divide the base into two sections: room without plants and room with plants.
  3. Make a small room with paper furniture like sofa, table, and window.
  4. Add cotton in the polluted side to show dirty air.
  5. Place air-purifying plants like Aloe Vera, Snake Plant, Areca Palm, Peace Lily, and Money Plant.
  6. Use arrows to show airflow (red arrows for polluted air and green arrows for clean air).
  7. Add labels showing CO₂ entering plants and O₂ being released.
  8. Attach a small information chart explaining plant benefits.

How It Works

Plants perform photosynthesis, where they absorb carbon dioxide (CO₂) and release oxygen (O₂). Some indoor plants can also absorb harmful chemicals from the air.

  • Leaves trap dust particles.
  • Plants absorb harmful gases like benzene and formaldehyde.
  • Roots and soil microorganisms break down pollutants.
  • The air becomes cleaner and healthier.

Expected Result

  • Room without plants shows polluted air.
  • Room with plants shows cleaner and fresher air.
  • Students understand how plants naturally purify air.

Conclusion

Air-purifying plants are a natural and eco-friendly solution to improve indoor air quality. They help reduce pollution, increase oxygen levels, and create a healthier living environment.

This project encourages people to grow more indoor plants in homes, schools, and offices to promote a greener and healthier future.
