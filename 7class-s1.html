Study of Factors Affecting Rusting of Iron

Back

Aim

To study the different factors affecting rusting of iron by observing rust formation in air, water, and salt water.

Concept

Rusting is a chemical reaction where iron reacts with oxygen and moisture to form iron oxide (rust).
Chemical Reaction: Iron + Oxygen + Water → Hydrated Iron Oxide (Rust)
Key Concepts: Chemical Reaction (Oxidation), Corrosion, Role of Oxygen and Water in rusting.

Materials Required

  • 4 clean iron nails
  • 4 test tubes or transparent plastic cups
  • Water
  • Salt
  • Cooking oil
  • Cotton
  • Sandpaper (to clean nails)
  • Labels
  • Test tube stand

Procedure / Steps

  1. Preparation: Clean all iron nails using sandpaper to remove any existing rust. Label the test tubes as A, B, C, and D.
  2. Setup the Experiment:
    • Test Tube A – Dry Air: Put a dry nail inside. Add cotton to absorb moisture. Keep it tightly closed.
    • Test Tube B – Water Only: Fill half the tube with water. Place a nail in it.
    • Test Tube C – Salt Water: Dissolve 1 spoon of salt in water. Pour into tube and place nail inside.
    • Test Tube D – Water with Oil Layer: Add water and insert nail. Pour a thin layer of oil on top to block air.
  3. Keep all tubes undisturbed for 3–5 days and record observations daily.

Observations

Test TubeConditionObservation
ADry AirNo rust formed
BWaterSlight rust
CSalt WaterHeavy rust formed
DWater + OilVery little or no rust

Result

  • Rusting requires both air (oxygen) and water.
  • Salt water increases the speed of rusting.
  • Without moisture or oxygen, rusting does not occur.

How It Works

Rusting is an oxidation reaction. Iron reacts with oxygen in the presence of water to form hydrated iron oxide (rust). Salt water increases electrical conductivity, making rusting faster.

Conclusion

  • Air and moisture are necessary for rusting.
  • Salt accelerates corrosion.
  • Preventing contact with air and water prevents rust.

Real Life Applications

  • Painting iron gates prevents rusting.
  • Oil coating on machines prevents corrosion.
  • Galvanization protects iron from rust.

Why This Model is Good for Exhibition

  • Easy to perform
  • Clear visible results
  • Strong chemical theory explanation
  • Related to daily life problems
