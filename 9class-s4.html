Hydraulic Robotic Arm

Back

Objective

  • To design and build a hydraulic robotic arm model using syringes and tubes.
  • To demonstrate how liquids transmit force based on Pascal’s Law.

Need for the Project

Hydraulic systems are widely used in many real-life machines and technologies such as:

  • Construction machines
  • Car braking systems
  • Industrial robots
  • Medical equipment

This model helps students understand:

  • How pressure works in liquids
  • How small force can lift heavy objects
  • Basic robotics and mechanical movement

Why It Is Impressive

  • Looks like a real industrial robot
  • Moves up, down, left, and right
  • Can grab and lift small objects
  • Demonstrates real-life physics concepts
  • Easy to build and explain

Materials Required

  • Cardboard or plywood base
  • 6–8 plastic syringes (without needles)
  • Plastic IV tubes or pipes
  • Water (with food color for visibility)
  • Glue gun or Fevicol
  • Ice-cream sticks or wooden strips
  • Nuts and bolts or pins (for joints)
  • Small plastic cup (for gripping claw)
  • Tape
  • Cutter and scissors

Steps to Make the Model

  1. Prepare the Base
    • Fix a strong cardboard or wooden board as the base.
    • Attach a vertical support for the arm.
  2. Make the Arm Structure
    • Use ice-cream sticks or wooden strips to create the base support, lower arm, upper arm, and claw.
    • Join parts using nuts, bolts, or pins to allow movement.
  3. Prepare the Hydraulic System
    • Fill syringes with colored water.
    • Remove air bubbles completely.
    • Connect two syringes using a plastic tube.
  4. Attach Syringes
    • Fix one syringe to the arm joint.
    • Keep the connected syringe in hand for control.
    • Repeat this for arm lifting, bending, and claw gripping.
  5. Testing
    • Push one syringe.
    • Observe the connected syringe moving.
    • The arm moves accordingly.

How It Works

The model works on Pascal's Law.

Pascal’s Law states that pressure applied to a confined liquid is transmitted equally in all directions.

  • When one syringe is pushed, pressure is created in the water.
  • The water transfers this pressure through the tube.
  • The piston of the second syringe moves.
  • This movement causes the robotic arm to lift or grab objects.

Since liquids are nearly incompressible, the force is transmitted efficiently.

Result / Output

  • The robotic arm moves smoothly using water pressure.
  • It can lift small objects like paper balls or erasers.
  • Clearly demonstrates hydraulic force.
  • Successfully proves Pascal’s Law.

Concepts Used

  • Pascal’s Law
  • Pressure in liquids
  • Hydraulics
  • Force transmission
  • Basic robotics

Conclusion

The Hydraulic Robotic Arm model demonstrates the practical application of Pascal’s Law in hydraulic systems. It helps students understand how liquids can transmit force and how hydraulic technology is used in modern machinery and robotics.
