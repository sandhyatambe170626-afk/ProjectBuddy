5. Impact of Noise Pollution on Humans and Animals

Back

Introduction

Noise pollution refers to unwanted or excessive sound that can disturb human life and the environment. In cities, noise is mainly produced by traffic, construction work, factories, loudspeakers, and fireworks.

Continuous exposure to loud noise can cause stress, hearing problems, sleep disturbance, and health issues in humans. It also affects animals and birds because they depend on sound for communication, navigation, and finding food.

Noise pollution is measured in decibels (dB). Sound above 85 dB can be harmful if a person is exposed to it for a a long time. Therefore, controlling noise pollution is important for maintaining a healthy environment.

Aim of the Project

The aim of this project is to study the sources of noise pollution and understand its harmful effects on humans and animals.

Vision of the Project

The vision of this project is to spread awareness about reducing noise pollution and creating a peaceful and healthy environment for humans and wildlife.

Materials Required

  • Notebook and pen
  • Mobile phone with sound meter app (optional)
  • Chart paper for recording data
  • Stopwatch
  • Observation sheet

Step-by-Step Procedure

  1. Visit different places such as roads, markets, schools, and parks.
  2. Observe and note the sources of noise like vehicles, construction work, or loudspeakers.
  3. Use a sound meter app on a mobile phone to measure the noise level in decibels (dB).
  4. Record the noise levels and compare them at different locations.
  5. Observe how noise affects people, birds, and animals in the area.
  6. Prepare a chart or table showing noise levels and their effects.

Scientific Reason

Sound travels in the form of waves through air. When the intensity of sound waves becomes too high, it can damage the sensitive cells in the human ear.

Excessive noise can cause:

  • Hearing loss
  • Stress and headaches
  • High blood pressure
  • Sleep disturbances

Animals and birds also use sound to communicate and detect danger. Loud noise can disturb their communication and cause them to leave their natural habitats.

Basic Scientific Concept

The intensity of sound is measured in decibels (dB).

Examples:

  • Normal conversation – 60 dB
  • Traffic noise – 80–90 dB
  • Fireworks – 120 dB

Sound intensity formula:

L = 10 log (I / I₀)

Where:

  • L = Sound level in decibels
  • I = Sound intensity
  • I₀ = Reference intensity

Effects of Noise Pollution

On Humans

  • Hearing loss
  • Stress and anxiety
  • Sleep problems
  • Difficulty in concentration

On Animals

  • Disturbs communication between animals
  • Causes stress and fear
  • Forces animals to leave their habitats

Ways to Reduce Noise Pollution

  • Avoid unnecessary honking
  • Use silencers in vehicles
  • Limit loudspeaker use
  • Plant more trees (they help absorb sound)
  • Follow noise control rules

Conclusion

Noise pollution is a serious environmental issue that affects both humans and animals. Continuous exposure to loud sounds can harm health and disturb natural ecosystems.

By following simple steps like reducing unnecessary noise, planting trees, and spreading awareness, we can help create a quieter and healthier environment for everyone.
